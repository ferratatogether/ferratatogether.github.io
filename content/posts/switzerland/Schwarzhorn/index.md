The Schwarzhorn Via Ferrata near Grindelwald is a moderately difficult (K3) alpine climb leading to the 2,928-meter summit of the Schwarzhorn, offering sweeping panoramic views of the Bernese Alps. Overall a very easy Ferrata (more like an Alpine path with some ladders).
Airily staggered aluminum ladders on steep vertical rock faces and an exposed ridge traverse

The route is a strict **one-way trail with no intermediate exits**. It ascends via the southwest ridge, leading climbers up a system of **airy, staggered vertical ladders** and continuous steel security cables. Halfway up the rock bands, a unique mountain resting bench chiseled into the ridge provides a panoramic rest stop before you cross the final knife-edge crest to reach the high summit cross.

## Recommended train 🚂: 
To get to the Schwarzhorn via ferrata (above Grindelwald) from Zurich by train, take an SBB train from Zürich HB to Interlaken Ost (via Bern), transfer to the regional train to Grindelwald, take local bus line 121 to the Firstbahn gondola, and ride up to First to begin the hike. Total travel time is around 3.5 to 4 hours one-way.

- Zürich HB to Interlaken Ost: Board the intercity train via Bern or Lucerne. Change trains at Interlaken Ost.
- Interlaken Ost to Grindelwald: Catch the Berner Oberland-Bahn (BOB) regional train directly to Grindelwald station.- Grindelwald to Firstbahn: Walk a short distance from the Grindelwald station to the local bus stop and take Bus 121 (direction: Oberer Gletscher) straight to the Grindelwald Firstbahn stop.
- Gondola to First: Ride the Grindelwald-First Gondola up to the top station at First, which serves as the base for the approach

From the First gondola exit, follow the wide gravel trail briefly descending toward Grosse Scheidegg past Distelboden. Turn left onto the marked mountain trail climbing up across the alpine meadows of Chrinnenboden. The path turns narrower and steeper into raw talus scree before winding into the **Grossi Chrinne (2,635m)** to access the rock entry cables.{{< /sac-scale >}}

{{< sac-scale level="T3" >}}
From the summit cross, follow the standard normal path dropping down along the narrow, exposed south ridge line. With views of the Bernese peaks and Lake Thun and Lake Brienz.
At the saddle before Gemschberg (2,659m), turn right to descend in sharp switchbacks down the loose, sliding gravel scree field into the basin below. Follow the path back past Chrinneboden to return to the First gondola terminal, or branch left to descend toward Grosse Scheidegg to catch the post bus.
A panoramic trail continues to Grosse Scheidegg, from where the post bus takes us back to Grindelwald.

## Water 🚰
Completely dry along the high rocky southwest ridge line. Pack a minimum of 1.5L of water to combat high-altitude fluid loss.

Get water or drinks at:
- Berggasthaus First (at the gondola top station terminal)
- Mountain kiosks in Grindelwald village

## Donation 💶
Access to the technical installations on the southwest ridge is completely free of charge. The route is meticulously maintained by the local Grindelwald mountain guides association and funded by the regional alpine cable networks.

## My review ⭐️
A premier, world-class high-altitude ridge adventure! While it relies more on classic high-alpine hiking footwork and mental composure than sheer athletic bicep strength (K2-K3), the exposure is real. Scaling the massive, staggered metal ladders feels like climbing straight into the sky, and standing on the 2,928m summit yields an absolutely jaw-dropping panoramic view over the Eiger, Mönch, Jungfrau, and the deep blue Oberland lakes! Sturdy high-top mountain boots are a must to safely handle the sliding gravel scree on the long south-ridge descent.
