## Route overview ℹ️

The Via Ferrata de Charmey is a modern, high-quality via ferrata network located on the steep slopes of the Dent de Vounetse (1,813m) in the Fribourg Prealps. Open seasonally from May to October, it features three distinct routes that cater to different skill levels—ranging from a fun intermediate climb to one of the most physically demanding, overhanging routes in Switzerland.

The routes share the same spectacular panoramic views of the Gastlosen, the Moléson, and the Gruyère region, but they diverge significantly in difficulty:

### Les Contreforts (K3 / K4)
The foothills route. It is accessible to intermediate climbers and features fun elements like ladders, a climbing net, and wire bridges. Note: It has some earthy passages, so it's best avoided right after heavy rain.
600 meters of steel cable (+180m elevation).

### Plein Vide I (K5)
A highly exposed, expert-level route. It handles a near-vertical cliff face through a narrow cave entrance and demands significant arm strength and a strong stomach for heights ("plein vide" translates to "open void").
660 meters of steel cable (+160m elevation).

### Plein Vide II (K6)
Widely considered the most challenging overhanging rock wall in Switzerland. It features a continuous series of aggressive overhangs and an optional pendulum swing. It is heavily endurance-focused and a rest lanyard ("vache") is absolutely essential
620 meters of steel cable (+160m elevation)

## By Car 🚗

Park or take public transit to the Vounetse gondola base station in the village of Charmey. Ride the cable car to the top station, followed by a panoramic, sporty 15-to-20-minute hike to the base of the rock face.

## Renting equipment 🛍️
If you bring your own gear, there is a CHF 4.00 maintenance contribution. Complete "all-in" day packages (Adults: CHF 39.00) include your round-trip gondola ticket, access fee, and full equipment rental (harness, helmet, and energy-absorbing lanyard) from the summit station.

TéléCharmey offers a combined package for CHF 57.00 that includes the gondola, gear rental, via ferrata access, and a 3-hour entry to the nearby Bains de la Gruyère thermal baths—perfect for soothing your forearms after wrestling the K5 or K6 routes
