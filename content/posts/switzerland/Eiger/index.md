## Route overview ℹ️
The 260 meter high route is suitable for people who are used to via ferrata climbing and for
experienced hikers with a head for heights. Be advised to only do via ferrata climbing when
equipped with necessary gear. The key spot is the vertical step immediately after the start of the
climb. In heavy rain, snow or icy conditions, you are strongly advised against climbing the via
ferrata! Rockfalls – as always around the Eiger! – cannot be ruled out!

- Kleine Scheidegg – Eigergletscher: approx. 1h
- Eigergletscher – Start: approx. 45mins
- Via ferrata: approx. 1h 15mins
- Descent – Eigergletscher: approx. 45mins
- Eigergletscher – Kleine Scheidegg: approx. 1h
- Kleine Scheidegg – Eigergletscher and back can also be done by mountain train.

## Weather ☀️

Weather can cancel/abort/shorten the event a few days before if weather is not perfect for execution! Given the severe high-alpine location, the limestone rock faces hold moisture long after rain, and climbing is highly dangerous during periods of snow, ice, or afternoon summer lightning storms.

From the Eigergletscher station platform exit, follow the world-famous Eiger Trail downwards briefly past the V-cableway tunnels. At the signposted mountain trail fork near the grassy hillock at Wart, turn sharply right to follow the singletrack leading over raw talus blocks up to the foot of the Rotstock rock spur (2,400m).{{< /sac-scale >}}

From the summit cross, walk back down to the shallow Rotstock saddle. From here, follow the white-blue-white painted trail markers descending across the steep, loose scree corridors and slippery slab sections of the western flank. Fixed safety ropes are present along tricky sections, bringing you straight back to the Eigergletscher complex.{{< /sac-scale >}}

## Water 🚰
Completely absent once you exit the train station building. Do not drink untreated stream water due to glacial silt. Pack a full hydration flask.

Get water or drinks at:
- Restaurant Eigergletscher (at the cable car mountain terminal)

## My review ⭐️
An atmospheric, world-class experience! Standing right on the edge of the notorious North Face feels absolutely legendary. Technically, it is a very friendly and secure climb (K2) that relies more on classic high-alpine hiking footwork than massive arm strength. However, do not underestimate the descent; the west flank is full of loose, sliding gravel talus that will challenge your balance when your knees are tired! Sturdy mountaineering boots are non-negotiable.
