## Route overview ℹ️

First documented in 1739 and fully modernized in 2007, Pinut is a fantastic, beginner-friendly route that scales the massive limestone walls of the **Flimserstein** plateau. The trail splits the ascent across three vertical rock terraces connected by lush high-alpine pastures:

### The Lower Wall

The most physically demanding stretch. Features a continuous system of wide iron ladders and a spectacular exposed wooden bridge. 

### The Caves & Tunnel

Pass through the iconic 20-meter-long natural rock tunnel—complete with a historic door—before stepping out onto the beautiful *Pinutwiese* hanging meadow.

### The Upper Terraces

Shorter, easier rock bands leading through the *Pardatsch* slopes to eventually emerge onto the wide alpine pastures of the summit plateau.

## Weather ☀️

Weather can cancel/abort/shorten the event a few days before if weather is not perfect for execution! The steel structures and limestone become exceptionally slick and dangerous when wet.

Take the InterCity train from Zürich HB down to Chur, switch directly to the regional Postbus line 81 to Flims Dorf, and transfer to the local connection ascending to the Fidaz trailhead.

## Cable cars 🚠
*No cable cars are required for this route.* The ascent is handled using local valley Postbuses and an purely pedestrian mountain approach path.

## By Car 🚗

Drive along the A13 motorway past Landquart toward Chur, take the exit heading to Tamins/Disentis, and follow the main cantonal road up the mountain flank to Flims and Fidaz.

## Parking 🅿️

Paid public car parks are available in Fidaz (including a dedicated lot near the historic milk cable car or at the edge of the forest). Space is tightly limited during sunny weekends.

## Water 🚰
Completely absent inside the rock tunnels and face sections. Bring a minimum of 1.5L of water to safely combat fluid loss.

Get water or drinks at:
- Self-catering honesty-box alpine hut at the top plateau
- Berghaus Bargis restaurant (located along the descent trail)

## Donation 💶
Access to the historic Pinut route is free of charge. However, the path relies wholly on donations to the *Association Pro Pinut* to fund the heavy winter upkeep and annual ladder structural checks.

## My review ⭐️
The absolute perfect choice for an introductory via ferrata! It offers an incredible ratio of massive exposure to very easy physical execution. Climbing up the 27 iron ladders feels historic and atmospheric, and moving through the natural cave tunnel is unforgettable. The view down across the turquoise waters of Caumasee and Crestasee is spectacular on clear autumn days.
