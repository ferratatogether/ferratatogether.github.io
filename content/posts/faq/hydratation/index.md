Dehydration affects both the body and the brain in measurable ways. Even a relatively small fluid loss (around 1–2% of body weight) can impair performance. 

## Effects on Body Performance
- Reduced endurance & strength – Muscle function decreases as dehydration impairs electrolyte balance and reduces blood flow.
- Early fatigue – Less plasma volume → heart works harder to circulate blood.
- Poor thermoregulation – Sweat rate drops, core temperature rises faster → risk of overheating.
- Slower reaction times – Muscles contract less efficiently when sodium, potassium, and magnesium are out of balance.
- Cramps & dizziness – Electrolyte and fluid loss disrupt neuromuscular control.

## Effects on Brain / Cognitive Function
- Impaired concentration & memory – Even mild dehydration can reduce attention, working memory, and short-term recall.
- Slower processing speed – Brain cells shrink slightly with water loss, impairing neural efficiency.
- Mood changes – Increased irritability, anxiety, and perception of task difficulty.
- Headaches & brain fog – Reduced cerebrospinal fluid volume and possible constriction of blood vessels.

## Decision-making decline
- Reduced executive function and problem-solving under stress.
Severity by % of Body Weight Lost (approximate effects)
- 1–2% → Thirst, reduced focus, slight performance decline.
- 3–4% → Noticeable fatigue, headaches, dizziness, reduced endurance.
- 5–6% → Confusion, heat exhaustion risk, major cognitive impairment.
- >10% → Hallucinations, risk of heatstroke, organ failure, potentially fatal.

Hydration needs vary by climate, activity level, and individual physiology, but consistent mild dehydration is one of the most common and overlooked causes of reduced physical and mental performance.
